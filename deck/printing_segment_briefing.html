01 · Method

A top-down method, applied the same way twice

PR

Public filings, nothing else

HP's own quarterly press releases for segment revenue and margin, SEC EDGAR 10-Q and 10-K filings (CIK 47217) for regional revenue, which never appears in a press release at all.

⇄

Two reporting bases, never mixed

HP restated FY24 and FY25 when it moved Print as a Service into Printing. The same quarter now has two published figures, kept side by side and never summed together.

✓

Eight validation checks

Internal consistency, matched against the exact change HP itself published when it restated older quarters, and two checks that test the segment and regional datasets against each other rather than against themselves.

↓

Broad to narrow, every layer

Total company first, then region and segment side by side, then business unit. Both dashboards in this project follow that exact shape, and so does this deck. Never built the other direction.

Printing's share of total HP revenue, Q1 FY24 to Q3 FY26. Personal Systems grew faster. This is a mix shift, not a Printing collapse.

05 · Surfaced while extracting the data

Three things noticed while pulling the numbers, not while reading the headlines

The analysis on every other slide is mine. These three were flagged by Claude Code during the extraction and reconciliation pass, worth showing separately rather than folding quietly into my own reading.

Consumer Printing

Units fell 9.0% while revenue fell only 1.8%, a roughly seven point gap. Both numbers only exist in the release's prose, never in a table, so this only turns up by reading the narrative carefully.

Currency

A weakening dollar added an estimated 1 to 2 points to Printing's reported growth in every FY26 quarter disclosed so far, found by comparing nominal growth against constant currency growth in the same release.

Cross-check

The FY26 restatement moved a loss making activity into Printing in the same move: revenue revised up, earnings before taxes revised down by 39 million across FY25. Only visible by reconciling two separately restated tables against each other, not from reading either one alone.
